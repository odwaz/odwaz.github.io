SpazaAPi

Online ordering and delivery for South African spaza shops: a storefront, a merchant dashboard and a driver flow on top of seven Spring Boot services that coordinate through Kafka. Payments arrive by webhook, stock and deliveries follow from events, and the whole thing is deployed to Amazon EKS in Cape Town.

Java 21 Spring Boot 3.4 Apache Kafka PostgreSQL Solo project: services, UI, infra and pipeline

Services, and how they hear about each other

Dashed boxes belong to other organisations. Payment providers call in with webhooks; a customer data platform receives activity and calls back with segment changes.

What it does

catalogue and stock

Merchants manage products and stock from the dashboard. Stock moves when order events arrive, not when a page is loaded.

payments

Card and instant-EFT payments through Stripe and Ozow, confirmed by webhook, plus vouchers and a wallet balance for repeat customers.

delivery

Drivers pick nearby jobs, share live location over WebSocket, and close each delivery with the customer's 4-digit PIN. Customers rate the driver afterwards.

pricing and routes

Delivery fees come from zones and distance, with peak-hour multipliers. Several orders can be grouped into one multi-stop route for a driver.

merchant analytics

Delivery success rates, delivery times and driver earnings over any date range.

CDP integration

Four services report customer activity to an external customer data platform. Its signed segment callbacks become signals the storefront and driver flow can act on.

Engineering highlights

What makes it hold up when payments, drivers and networks misbehave.

Services hear about each other through events

An order publishes to order-events; catalog adjusts stock and delivery creates the job, each in its own consumer group. Delivery publishes to delivery-events, and order follows along. No service calls another to finish its own work.

Every consumer assumes it will see things twice

Kafka and payment providers both deliver at least once. Delivery skips an order that already has a delivery, order never moves a status backwards, and the Ozow and Stripe handlers skip transactions they've already processed.

Deliveries that recover on their own

Scheduled sweeps handle the real world. An assignment a driver hasn't picked up in 30 minutes goes back to the pool, a driver silent for 5 minutes is marked offline, and status updates that failed to send are retried from a queue every 2 minutes for up to 24 hours.

Proof at the door

A delivery only completes with the PIN the customer received, so "delivered" means the customer had the parcel in hand, not that the driver tapped a button.

Reactions to segment changes, verified

customer-service verifies each CDP callback's Ed25519 signature, then maps the segment to a signal: a browsing nudge, a support flag for delivery trouble, or a wallet suggestion for cash payers. Signals clear when the customer leaves the segment.

Run like production

On AWS it runs on EKS in af-south-1 with RDS PostgreSQL, an NLB in front of NGINX ingress, and DNS in Route 53. PodDisruptionBudgets keep services up through node drains, and Prometheus, Grafana, Loki and Jaeger cover metrics, logs and traces. Terraform defines the AWS and Huawei Cloud environments.
